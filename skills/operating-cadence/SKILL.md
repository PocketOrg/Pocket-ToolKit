# Operating Cadence

## A cadence exists to make decisions, not to share information

Any meeting whose output is that everyone now knows something should have been a document. The meetings worth holding are the ones where a decision gets made or a disagreement gets resolved.

Every recurring meeting needs a stated decision it exists to make. Those that cannot name one get cancelled, and almost nothing happens.

## Separate the horizons, or they collide

Weekly is execution: what is blocked, what changed, who needs what. Monthly is performance against plan. Quarterly is direction. Mixing them means either strategy displaced by an urgent operational issue, or an operational blocker waiting a quarter.

Different cadence, different attendees, different preparation.

## Written before, discussed during

A document circulated in advance, read before the meeting, turns an hour of presentation into an hour of argument about the real question. Presenting live guarantees the reading happens at the speed of the slowest reader and nobody challenges anything.

Silent reading at the start is better than hoping. It costs ten minutes and it works.

## A decision without a name and a date is not a decision

Record what was decided, who owns it, when it lands, and — most valuably — what was decided *against* and why. Six weeks later the same option returns and the reasoning is gone.

Revisit deliberately when something changes, not because someone forgot.

## Watch out for

- A standing agenda nobody has questioned in a year.
- Attendees who never speak, which is either a signal about the meeting or about them.
- Metrics reported because they are available rather than because they drive a decision.
- Actions carried forward repeatedly, which usually means unowned or too large.
- Escalation happening in side channels because the cadence is too slow.

## Finishing

Every recurring meeting names the decision it exists to make. Horizons are separated by cadence and attendees. Material is written and read before discussion. Decisions record owner, date and the rejected alternative.
