# Property and Facilities Management

## Triage by consequence, not by who complained loudest

Safety and habitability first: no heat, no water, electrical fault, anything that makes a home unsafe or legally uninhabitable. Then things that get worse if left — a leak damages structure at a rate a dripping tap does not. Then everything else.

Publish the categories and the target response for each. Most tenant frustration is about not knowing when, not about waiting.

## Preventive work is cheaper, and the arithmetic is not close

A serviced boiler and a cleared gutter cost a fraction of the failure they prevent, and the failure arrives with emergency call-out rates and consequential damage.

Keep a calendar per asset with its service interval, and treat a missed service as an incident. Deferred maintenance is borrowing at a rate nobody records.

## Compliance dates are hard dates

Gas, electrical, fire safety, legionella, asbestos, insurance — each with its own interval and its own evidence requirement. Late is not a paperwork problem; it can void insurance and, if something happens, it is the first thing examined.

Track them with a reminder well before expiry, because contractors are not always available on your timeline.

## Document at the time, in the tenant's presence where possible

Photographs, dates, what was said, what was agreed. Deposit disputes and access disagreements are decided on records, and a record written weeks later carries little weight.

## Watch out for

- Access notice periods shortened for convenience, which is often unlawful.
- One contractor with no alternative for an urgent trade.
- Deposit deductions with no condition evidence from move-in.
- Recurring faults treated as separate jobs, hiding a single underlying cause.
- Communal areas with no named responsibility.

## Finishing

Requests are categorised by consequence with published response targets. Every asset has a service interval and a calendar. Compliance dates are tracked with early reminders. Records were made at the time.
