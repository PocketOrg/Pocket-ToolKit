# Creative Direction

## A direction is a set of constraints, not a mood board

"Modern and friendly" constrains nothing and every reviewer reads it differently. A usable direction says what is in and — more importantly — what is out: no stock photography, one accent colour, sentence case throughout, no drop shadows.

Constraints are what let several people produce work that looks like one thing.

## Brief for the decision the work must survive

Who sees this, where, in what state of mind, and what must they do next. A billboard glanced at from a car and a landing page read at a desk are different problems, and a brief that omits this gets solved for the wrong one.

State the one thing that must land if everything else is missed.

## Critique the work against the brief, not against taste

Useful feedback names the brief requirement it fails: "the offer is the third thing you read and it needs to be first". Unhelpful feedback names a preference: "I don't love the blue".

Where the note *is* taste, say so honestly — "this is preference, treat it as optional" — and let the maker decide. Unlabelled preference is what turns three rounds into nine.

## Converge deliberately

Round one explores; round two narrows to a direction; round three refines. Reopening the direction at round three is the single largest cause of creative overrun, and it is nearly always caused by someone joining late.

Name who approves before round one and hold to it.

## Watch out for

- Feedback aggregated from a committee with no reconciliation, producing contradictory notes.
- Accessibility and contrast treated as a compliance pass rather than a design constraint.
- Work reviewed at the wrong size or on the wrong medium.
- The strongest idea killed because it was unfamiliar rather than wrong.
- A direction that lives only in one person's head and cannot be applied without them.

## Finishing

The direction states what is out as well as in. Briefs name the audience, context and the one thing that must land. Notes cite the brief or are labelled preference. Approval was named before round one.
