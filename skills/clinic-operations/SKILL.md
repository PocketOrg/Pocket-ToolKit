# Clinic Operations

## Clinical time is the constraint, and everything else exists to protect it

Every administrative decision either adds to or removes from time with patients. Measure how much of a clinician's day is spent on things someone else could do — and it is usually far more than anyone assumes.

That is the frame for every process question here.

## The schedule fails at the joins

Overrunning appointments cascade, and the last patient of the session absorbs the whole day's slippage. Fixed slot lengths for genuinely variable consultations guarantee this.

Match slot length to appointment type, keep deliberate catch-up gaps, and measure actual duration by type rather than defending the nominal figure.

## No-shows are a system output

Charging for them mostly moves the cost to the people least able to bear it. What actually reduces them: reminders at a useful interval, easy rescheduling, and knowing which appointment types and times fail most.

Look for the pattern before the penalty. A slot nobody can reach by public transport at 9am is not a patient-motivation problem.

## Handle data as if it will be audited, because it may be

Access on a need-to-know basis, audited. Consent recorded for what it actually covers. Retention and deletion on a schedule. Never patient-identifiable information in an unsecured channel, however convenient.

A practice's data handling is the part patients cannot check and most rely on.

## Watch out for

- Results or referrals with no closed loop, so nobody notices one was never actioned.
- Reception carrying clinical triage informally, without training or protocol.
- Recall lists maintained by hand and quietly falling behind.
- Interpreting or accessibility needs recorded but not surfaced at booking.
- Complaints resolved individually with no pattern review.

## Finishing

Administrative load on clinical time is measured. Slot lengths match measured durations by type. No-show patterns were examined before penalties. Data access is need-to-know and audited. Results and referrals have closed loops.
