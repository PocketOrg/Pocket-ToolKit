# Customer Retention

## Churn is decided long before the cancellation

By the time someone asks to leave, the decision is usually weeks old and the conversation is a formality. Saving accounts at that point is expensive and mostly fails.

So the work is early: noticing the account that stopped using the thing it bought, whose champion left, whose usage halved. Those are the intervention points.

## Usage is the signal; satisfaction surveys are not

A customer can report satisfaction and not renew, and can complain constantly while renewing every year. What predicts renewal is whether they are getting the value they bought — visible in usage, in which features, by how many people.

Define the two or three behaviours that mean "getting value" for your product, and watch those.

## The renewal conversation is about outcomes achieved

Not features shipped and not relationship. What did they set out to do, what happened, and what is next. That framing also surfaces honestly when the answer is "nothing much", which is information you need before the renewal date rather than at it.

## Ask why, and believe the answer

Departing customers will usually tell you plainly if you ask without arguing. Record the reason in their words, not the category you would have chosen.

Aggregate them. Three accounts leaving for the same fixable reason is a roadmap item; treated individually it stays invisible.

## Watch out for

- A single champion, whose departure ends the account with no warning.
- Discounting a renewal without addressing why they were leaving — it buys one cycle.
- Support tickets resolved individually with no pattern review.
- Onboarding declared complete when setup finished rather than when value started.
- Health scores computed from fields nobody maintains.

## Finishing

Value behaviours are defined and watched. Intervention happens on usage signals, not on notice. Renewals discuss outcomes achieved. Departure reasons are recorded verbatim and aggregated.
