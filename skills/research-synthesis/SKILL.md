# Research Synthesis

## The question comes first, in a form that can be answered

"What do we know about pricing" produces a reading list. "Would a 10% price rise on our top three lines lose more revenue than it gains, over one quarter" produces a decision.

Write the question so that a possible answer would change what somebody does. If no answer would change anything, the research is not worth doing yet.

## Weight sources, do not merely count them

Ten blog posts citing one study is one study. A primary source beats a summary of it, a large sample beats a small one, a pre-registered result beats a retrospective one, and a source with an interest in the conclusion needs corroboration.

Note the sample, the method and the date on anything load-bearing. A robust 2015 finding about consumer behaviour may simply no longer hold.

## Conflicting evidence is a finding, not a problem to resolve

When good sources disagree, say so and say why: different populations, different definitions, different periods. Averaging them produces a number that describes nothing.

The honest output is often "the evidence supports X in these conditions and Y in those", which is more useful than false certainty.

## Separate what the evidence says from what you conclude

Two sections, always. The evidence is checkable; the recommendation is judgement built on it plus context the sources do not have. Merging them makes the recommendation look like a finding and stops anyone challenging it.

## Watch out for

- Searching until you find agreement, then stopping.
- A citation that does not actually say what it is cited for — check the passage, not the title.
- Absence of evidence reported as evidence of absence.
- Percentages without a base, and changes without a starting point.
- A single striking anecdote outweighing a duller dataset.

## Finishing

The question is answerable and decision-relevant. Sources are weighted with sample, method and date. Conflicts are stated with their reasons. Evidence and recommendation are in separate sections.
