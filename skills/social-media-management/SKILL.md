# Social Media Management

## Posting frequency is not the strategy

A full calendar of forgettable posts trains an audience to scroll past you. One thing a week that is genuinely worth someone's attention outperforms daily filler on every metric that matters.

Decide what you have to say that nobody else can. If the answer is nothing this week, post nothing.

## Each platform is a different medium

The same message needs different form, length and register per platform, and cross-posting identical content signals that none of them matter to you. Native format wins: vertical video where that is the norm, a real thread where that is the norm.

Pick the two platforms where your audience actually is and do them properly, rather than six badly.

## Measure the metric that connects to the business

Impressions and follower count move for reasons unrelated to outcomes. Saves, shares, profile visits and click-through connect to intent; conversions connect to revenue.

Report the connected ones. A post with 50,000 impressions and no clicks was entertainment you paid for.

## Reply, and reply as a person

The comments are the product on social. An account that broadcasts and never answers is a billboard with extra steps. Fast, specific, human replies build more than the posts do.

Never argue in public. Move a genuine dispute to a private channel in one reply and resolve it there.

## Watch out for

- Scheduled posts running through a crisis or a national event — always have a pause procedure.
- Trend participation with no connection to what you do, which reads as desperate.
- Engagement bait that briefly works and trains the algorithm to show you to the wrong people.
- User content reposted without permission or credit.
- One person holding every account password with no recovery path.

## Finishing

Frequency follows having something to say. Content is native per platform, on the two that matter. Reporting uses intent and revenue metrics. Replies happen fast and as a person. A pause procedure exists.
