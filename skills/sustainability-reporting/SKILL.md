# Sustainability Reporting

## Measure before committing to anything

A target set without a baseline is a press release. You cannot report progress against a number you never established, and retrofitting a baseline later invites exactly the scepticism the report was meant to avoid.

Establish the boundary — which entities, which sites, which period — and hold it constant, or every year-on-year comparison is meaningless.

## Know which scope a number belongs to

Direct emissions, purchased energy, and everything in the value chain are different quantities with different data quality. The third is usually the largest and the least certain, which is precisely why it gets omitted.

Report it with its uncertainty rather than leaving it out. An incomplete figure honestly labelled is far more credible than a tidy one that excludes most of the impact.

## Estimated is not the same as measured, and both are legitimate

Metered consumption is measured. A spend-based emissions factor is an estimate with a wide error. Both belong in a report; conflating them does not.

Label the method per figure. A reader who cannot tell which is which will discount all of it.

## Never claim neutrality you cannot evidence

"Carbon neutral", "sustainable", "eco-friendly" attract regulatory attention and are the claims most often challenged. Offsets have quality and permanence questions that a certificate does not settle.

Say what you did: reduced this by that much, measured this way, offset the remainder with this instrument. Specific and dull survives; broad and confident does not.

## Watch out for

- Boundary changed between years, making an improvement out of a divestment.
- Intensity metrics used to show progress while absolute emissions rose.
- Supplier data accepted unverified where it dominates the total.
- A target with no interim milestones, so nobody can tell if it is on track.
- Selective reporting of the categories that improved.

## Finishing

A baseline and a constant boundary exist. Every scope is reported, including the incomplete one with its uncertainty. Each figure is labelled measured or estimated. Claims state what was actually done.
