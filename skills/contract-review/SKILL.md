# Contract Review

## Read for failure, not for meaning

Anyone can read what a contract says when things go well. The value is in what happens when they do not: who bears the loss, who can walk away, what it costs to leave, and who decides.

Go to liability, indemnity, termination, and dispute resolution first. Those four decide the actual exposure.

## Unlimited liability is the clause to find first

Caps, exclusions and carve-outs from the cap are where a commercially small deal becomes an unbounded one. Read the carve-outs especially: a cap with broad exceptions is not a cap.

Check the cap against the contract value. Unlimited liability on a low-value agreement is rarely a deal anyone would sign deliberately.

## Termination is about cost, not about rights

Everyone has a right to terminate somewhere. What matters is notice period, what survives, whether fees continue, whether you get your data back and in what format, and how long transition takes.

Auto-renewal with a short cancellation window is the most commonly overlooked trap and the easiest to fix.

## Distinguish what you would change from what you must

A review producing forty comments gets ignored or delays the deal. Rank: must-change to sign, should-change if possible, noted-and-accepted.

Naming what you are accepting is itself the deliverable — it means the risk was chosen rather than missed.

## Watch out for

- Governing law and jurisdiction making enforcement impractical whatever the terms say.
- Indemnities that are not mutual where the risk is.
- IP ownership of things created under the contract left ambiguous.
- Data-processing terms absent where personal data is obviously involved.
- Incorporated documents by reference — the linked policy the other side can change unilaterally.
- A signature block naming an entity that is not the one with the assets.

## Finishing

Liability, indemnity, termination and disputes were read first. Caps were checked against value including carve-outs. Comments are ranked must / should / accepted. Accepted risks are named explicitly.
