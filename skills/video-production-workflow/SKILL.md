# Video Production Workflow

## Everything expensive is decided before the camera is on

The re-shoot is the only genuinely unrecoverable cost in video. It is nearly always caused by something knowable in advance: no shot list, unclear approval, an unbooked location, missing consent.

Lock the edit's structure before shooting. Shooting to find the story means covering everything, which is how a one-day shoot becomes three.

## Sound is the half people notice

Audiences forgive soft focus and forgive grade. They stop watching bad audio within seconds. Budget and attention should follow that, which usually means a separate recorder, a lav on anyone speaking, and thirty seconds of room tone in every location.

Record room tone. The edit will need it and you cannot fake it afterwards.

## Name and back up at the card, not in the edit

Files get renamed once, at ingest, to a scheme that survives being seen out of context: date, project, camera, card, take. Two copies before the card is formatted, one of them off-site.

A project lost to a single drive is a project lost entirely, and it happens to people who knew better.

## Review in passes, not all at once

Structure first — is this the right order and length. Then picture. Then sound. Then grade and titles. Mixing the passes produces notes on a colour grade for a scene that gets cut, which wastes the reviewer's attention and the editor's day.

Give each review a named question and a deadline.

## Watch out for

- Music without a licence that covers the actual distribution.
- Appearance releases missing for anyone identifiable, which surfaces at publication.
- Aspect ratio and captions decided after the edit rather than before framing.
- Approval by a group with no named decider.
- No archive of the finished project's source files and edit data.

## Finishing

The edit structure was agreed before the shoot. Audio was recorded separately with room tone. Files are named to a scheme with two copies. Every review pass had one question.
