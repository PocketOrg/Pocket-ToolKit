# Event Planning and Run of Show

## The run of show is the deliverable

Not the plan, not the deck: a minute-by-minute document naming what happens, who does it, and who decides if it slips. On the day, nobody reads anything else.

Include the transitions. Events run late in the gaps between items, not during them.

## Every dependency has an owner and a deadline that is not the event

Catering, AV, venue access, permits, signage, speakers' materials. A dependency whose deadline is "the event" is a dependency that will be discovered on the day.

Work backwards from the door opening, and put the AV test earlier than feels necessary. It is always the thing that fails.

## Contingency is specific, not a percentage

"Ten per cent buffer" is not a plan. What if the keynote does not arrive, the stream drops, it rains, twice the expected number turn up, half do. Each needs a named response and someone empowered to trigger it.

Write the three most likely failures and their responses on the run of show itself.

## Attendance is a forecast, and no-show rates are knowable

Free events no-show heavily; paid ones much less. Catering, seating and staffing all key off that number, and guessing it is how you either waste half the food or run out in twenty minutes.

Use your own past rate where you have one. Say which figure you used and why.

## Watch out for

- Accessibility treated as a late addition rather than a venue-selection criterion.
- Speakers who have not seen the actual stage timing or been told their real slot.
- One person holding every supplier relationship in their own inbox.
- A budget with no line for overtime, damage or last-minute AV.
- Nobody assigned to the door in the first fifteen minutes, which is when arrival peaks.

## Finishing

A minute-by-minute run of show names owners and deciders, including transitions. Every dependency has a pre-event deadline. The three likeliest failures have written responses. Attendance uses a real no-show rate.
