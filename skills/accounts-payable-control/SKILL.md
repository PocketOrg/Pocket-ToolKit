# Accounts Payable Control

## Three-way match, and why it is not bureaucracy

Purchase order, goods received note, invoice. Each answers a different question: was it authorised, did it arrive, is this what we agreed to pay. Skipping any one of them is how a business pays for things it did not order or did not receive.

Where a match is impossible — services, subscriptions — name the substitute control rather than waiving the requirement silently.

## Segregation is the control that actually works

Whoever creates a supplier must not be whoever approves a payment. Most payment fraud is a new bank account on an existing supplier, and that single separation defeats it.

Verify bank-detail changes by calling a number you already held, never one from the request itself.

## Duplicates are structural, not careless

The same invoice arrives by email and by post, or with a leading zero dropped, or split across two POs. Detection keys on supplier plus amount plus date and near-matches of the reference — not exact-match on the invoice number, which is what lets them through.

## A payment run is a decision about cash, not a task

Prioritise by consequence: statutory obligations, then supply-critical suppliers, then everything else. Take the early-settlement discount when it beats the cost of the cash, and say so with the arithmetic.

Sitting on an invoice without telling the supplier is a decision too — an expensive one, in goodwill.

## Watch out for

- Round-sum invoices with vague descriptions from suppliers nobody recognises.
- Approval thresholds that are just below the level requiring a second signature.
- Credit notes not applied, so the balance is paid twice over.
- Statements never reconciled to the ledger, hiding both missing invoices and duplicates.
- Urgency as a pressure tactic. Genuine urgency survives a verification call.

## Finishing

Every payment traces to an authorisation and a receipt or a named substitute control. Bank changes were verified independently. Duplicate checks ran on near-matches, not exact ones.
