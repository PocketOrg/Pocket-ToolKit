# Grant Writing and Reporting

## Choose which to apply for before writing anything

An application takes real hours and displaces delivery. Score candidates on fit with what you already do, size against effort, restriction terms, and whether the reporting burden is survivable.

A grant that pays for work you would not otherwise do is a strategy change wearing a funding disguise. Sometimes right — but decide it deliberately.

## Answer the question they asked, in their words

Funders read many applications against a rubric. Reusing a generic case for support and hoping it maps costs you the easy marks. Use their terminology, their outcome framing, their section order.

Where you cannot answer well, say so briefly rather than padding. Assessors recognise padding and it costs more than the gap.

## Outcomes are what changed, not what you did

"Ran twelve workshops" is an activity. "Sixty-two participants, of whom forty-one entered work within six months, measured by follow-up survey at 71% response" is an outcome with its own limitation stated.

Name the measurement method and its weaknesses. An outcome with no method is not evidence, and assessors know it.

## Budget honestly, including overheads

Under-costing to look efficient means delivering at a loss and being unable to report success. Include the staff time, the management cost and the reporting effort the grant itself creates.

If a funder disallows overheads, that is a real cost of that money — price it into the decision rather than absorbing it invisibly.

## Report the shortfall before you are asked

A programme that missed its target and says so, with cause and what changed, keeps its funder. One that reframes the miss loses trust permanently, and funders talk to each other.

## Watch out for

- Restricted funds spent on general costs, which is the fastest route to losing a funder.
- Outcomes promised at a scale the staffing cannot reach.
- The same beneficiaries counted in several programmes' numbers.
- Reporting deadlines missed on renewals that were otherwise certain.
- A dependence ratio nobody has calculated — one funder at 60% is a strategic risk.

## Finishing

The application was chosen against explicit criteria. Answers use the funder's framing. Outcomes state measurement method and limitations. The budget includes overheads and reporting effort.
