# Payroll Operations

## Wrong pay is not a data problem

An underpayment is somebody's rent. An overpayment is a recovery conversation nobody wants and, in many places, one you cannot simply reverse from the next payslip without consent.

So the checks belong before the run, not in a reconciliation afterwards. Payroll is the one process where "we will correct it next cycle" is a real cost to a real person.

## Compare every run to the last one

The highest-yield control is a variance report against the previous period, line by line, per person. New starters, leavers, changed hours, changed tax codes and one-off payments should account for every difference.

A difference you cannot attribute is an error you have not found yet.

## Statutory deductions are not negotiable or memorable

Rates, thresholds, contribution bands and filing dates change by year and jurisdiction. Look them up for the period being run. Never carry a rate forward because it was right last quarter.

## Corrections have a right order

Establish the correct gross first, then the correct deductions, then the difference to pay or recover, then the statutory correction filing. Adjusting net pay directly leaves the deduction records wrong, which surfaces months later as a mismatch with the authority.

Tell the person before the money moves. Every time.

## Watch out for

- A leaver paid a full period, or a starter missed entirely, because the cutoff was ambiguous.
- Backdated pay rises spanning a tax-year boundary.
- Benefits in kind and expense reimbursements treated as the same thing.
- Bank details changed shortly before a run — a common fraud pattern, and worth a call-back to a known number.
- One person holding the whole process with no documented runbook.

## Finishing

Every variance from last period is attributed. Statutory rates were verified for this period. Anyone affected by a correction was told before it happened.
